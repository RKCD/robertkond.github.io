Problems I help solve

Two clocks, and the gap between them

Technology moves on an exponential clock. People, organizations, institutions and physical infrastructure adapt more slowly. The value is not only in the new technology. It is in the bridges that help an organization adapt sooner.

A

A change is coming, but its impact is unclear

  • A new regulation or standard.
  • Technology changes how the work gets done.
  • New duties or risks appear, and no one knows what to do first.

Possible resultImpact analysis, a map of obligations, an action plan and the information needed to decide.

B

The work is manual and scattered

  • Analysing the same kinds of documents again and again.
  • Collecting information from different systems.
  • Reporting, checks and putting evidence together.
  • Knowledge that doesn’t move between people and teams.

Possible resultAn automated workflow, connected data, an AI agent or an internal tool.

C

AI is available, but using it reliably is hard

  • What an AI agent may and may not do.
  • Human oversight and who is accountable.
  • Results you can check, with sources.
  • AI inside a real work process, not a demo.

Possible resultA verifiable AI workflow, a map of risks and accountability, a prototype.

How we start

Change-to-Action Sprint

Find out what a change requires of your organization, and what is worth building to deal with it.

Together we

  1. Map one specific technological, regulatory or market change.
  2. Work out who it affects and which duties, risks or opportunities it creates.
  3. Put together a prioritized action plan with owners, deadlines and sources.
  4. Find one repetitive or time-consuming task that AI or automation could improve.
  5. If it makes sense, build the first working prototype or automated workflow.

You get

  • A clear view of what the change means for you
  • A prioritized action plan
  • Sources and reasoning behind the key conclusions
  • A proposal for a technical solution
  • A working prototype, if we agree on one

What I’ve built

Tools you can check

Everything that has to be right lives in code or in checked rules. The model handles language.

EU AI Act Readiness Check

Problem
The AI Act deadlines moved in July 2026. I asked three models about them, and all three gave the same wrong answer.
Approach
Separate what needs judgement (which category fits this system) from what has to be exact (dates and legal basis).
Solution
Describe your AI system and get its risk category, your role, and the obligations that apply, with dates and sources. Exports to PDF.
Architecture
The model only picks categories from a fixed list. Code adds the date, the article, the act that changed it and what the date used to be, from one checked legal corpus.
Verifiable
Five test cases, from CV screening to a spam filter, check every result (5 of 5 passed on 24 August 2026). Each answer shows when the corpus was last checked by hand and warns when it is out of date. Every month a scheduled agent looks for new amending acts. It can report, not edit.
Status
Live · access by invitation · the first load can take up to a minute
Result
No usage measured yet.

Agent liability map for Estonia

Problem
When an AI agent gets something wrong, it is unclear which rules apply, who is liable and where to report it.
Approach
Seven concrete situations, such as a payment, personal data, a decision about an employee or false information to a customer.
Solution
For each situation: the Estonian and EU rules that apply, who answers, where to report, and what to keep right away.
Architecture
All content sits in one data file. The build refuses to publish a rule without a source link and status. Agents may propose changes. The page changes only when a person approves them.
Verifiable
Rules link to the primary text in Riigi Teataja or the EU Publications Office. Checked rules carry a date, unchecked ones are marked. Four of the seven situations are fully checked.
Status

Opportunity radar

Changes worth acting on

Not a news feed. For each change: who it affects, what gap it opens, and what you could do next. Dates come from the source act.

AI agents in everyday work

AI agents can take on longer, multi-step work tasks. Organizations are starting to hand them repetitive processes.

Who it affects
Organizations automating repetitive processes, and the companies that supply the agents.
The gap
No clear rights, limits, owners or checks. When an agent errs, no one knows who answers.
Opportunity
A control and accountability layer for agents: a mandate, a log and a person who signs off.
Next step
Before deploying an agent, write down what it may do, how much, for how long, and who checks. Liability map

New product liability rules cover software, including AI, for products placed on the market after 9 Dec 2026 · Directive (EU) 2024/2853 of 23 Oct 2024 · checked 21 Oct 2026

Energy agents need a receipt

Software that decides on its own when to heat or charge a battery is already used in homes and companies. It can sell flexibility to the grid without anyone pressing a button.

Who it affects
Flexibility aggregators, device makers, building owners and their insurers.
The gap
Trust. There is no receipt: what the agent was allowed to do, what it did and what came of it.
Opportunity
A verifiable, timestamped record of every automated decision.
Next step
List the decisions your system makes on its own, and what you could show a customer afterwards.

AI Act: high-risk deadline moved, transparency already applies

The Digital Omnibus moved the obligations for high-risk AI (Annex III) from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027. The transparency duties in Article 50 were not moved and have applied since 2 Aug 2026.

Who it affects
Organizations that use AI in hiring, education, credit and other Annex III areas, and their suppliers.
The gap
A later date reads as “nothing to do yet”. Risk management, documentation and human oversight take time to build into a running process.
Opportunity
Use the extra time to list your AI systems and build oversight into the process once.
Next step
List where you use AI and run each system through the readiness check.

Annex III obligations apply from 2 Dec 2027 · Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 Jul 2026 · checked 24 Aug 2026

EU AI Act Readiness Check

Probleem
AI Acti tähtajad muutusid 2026. aasta juulis. Küsisin nende kohta kolmelt mudelilt ja kõik kolm andsid sama vale vastuse.
Lähenemine
Eraldada see, mis vajab hinnangut (milline kategooria sellele süsteemile sobib), sellest, mis peab olema täpne (kuupäevad ja õiguslik alus).
Lahendus
Kirjelda oma AI-süsteemi ja saad selle riskikategooria, oma rolli ning kehtivad kohustused koos kuupäevade ja allikatega. Tulemuse saab PDF-ina.
Arhitektuur
Mudel valib ainult kategooriaid kindlast nimekirjast. Kood lisab kuupäeva, artikli, seda muutnud akti ja varasema kuupäeva ühest kontrollitud õiguskorpusest.
Kontrollitavus
Viis testjuhtumit, CV-de sõelumisest rämpsposti filtrini, kontrollivad iga tulemust (24. augustil 2026 läbisid kõik viis). Iga vastus näitab, millal korpust viimati käsitsi kontrolliti, ja hoiatab, kui see on aegunud. Iga kuu otsib ajastatud agent uusi muutmisakte. Ta saab teatada, mitte muuta.

Agentide vastutuse kaart Eestis

Probleem
Kui AI-agent eksib, on ebaselge, millised reeglid kehtivad, kes vastutab ja kuhu teatada.
Lähenemine
Seitse konkreetset olukorda, näiteks makse, isikuandmed, töötajat puudutav otsus või kliendile antud vale info.
Lahendus
Iga olukorra kohta: kehtivad Eesti ja EL-i reeglid, kes vastutab, kuhu teatada ja mida kohe säilitada.
Arhitektuur
Kogu sisu on ühes andmefailis. Ehitus keeldub avaldamast normi, millel pole allikalinki ja staatust. Agendid võivad muudatusi pakkuda, aga leht muutub alles siis, kui inimene need kinnitab.
Kontrollitavus
Normid viitavad esmaallikale Riigi Teatajas või ELi väljaannete talituses. Kontrollitud normi juures on kuupäev, kontrollimata norm on märgitud. Seitsmest olukorrast neli on täielikult kontrollitud.
